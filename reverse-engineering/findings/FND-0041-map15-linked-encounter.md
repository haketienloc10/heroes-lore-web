# FND-0041 — Map-15 linked `az`/`aj`/`cf` encounter

## Question

How does the map-15 loader construct enemies 39–41, how do the two attackers and linked hazard act, and what happens when the hazard dies?

## Reference and method

- Reference JAR: `original/heroes-lore-v2.1.2.jar`, SHA-256 `7eb032297967f5d8382cc4f1ab5c3016459e01b235b42a3b88c6a8bfe46c5f61`.
- Read JADX output for `ad`, `az`, `aj`, `cf`, `au`, and `ak`; cross-checked the map loader and encounter methods with `javap -classpath original/heroes-lore-v2.1.2.jar -c -p ad az aj cf au ak`. Method-relative bytecode offsets are recorded below.
- Checked map 15's extracted event table, the three `/enm/data0` records, and `/boss/spr/3_39`, `/boss/spr/3_40`, and `/boss/spr/3_41` animation tracks.

## Verified source facts

- `ad.h()` loads enemy records 39, 40, and 41 into local sprite slots 0, 1, and 2 (bytecode 40–79). `ad.i()` constructs `az` (enemy 40, `(0,12)`, width 2), then `aj` (enemy 41, `(13,11)`, width 1), then linked `cf` (enemy 39, `(7,7)`, width 2; bytecode 0–92). The extracted ordinary map-15 event table has no placements for these IDs.
- `az.d()` and `aj.d()` move vertically by 8 pixels in state 2 (bytecode 39–115 and 97–175). `az.i()` selects its attack window and target range (18–53), while `az.j()` sets camera offsets on frames 6–8 (8–78), resolves the frame-five strike callback (81–181), then resets its lifetime counter and restores its mutable action delay to 2. `aj.i()` selects its gated attack and alternates direction through `w` (18–73); `aj.j()` resolves the separate phase-one frame-5 and phase-two frame-8 ranges (4–208). The phase-two hit callback resets its lifetime counter and restores the action delay to 2.
- `cf.d()` delegates the ordinary actor update; `cf.h()`/`cf.i()` handle entry to its cadence and the three-attack/40-update pause (method-relative offsets 0–47 in `cf.i()`). `cf.j()` advances the four-cell hazard row over x=6–9 and rows y through y+4 (switch at 8–19, per-row helper calls at 56–122); its private `cf.a(an,ad,byte)` helper applies the early-hit 16-pixel downward displacement and full attack (offsets 0–37). At update 12, `cf.j()` checks x=6–9 and y+5 through y+8 and applies half attack without the displacement (216–229). `cf` remains stationary.
- `cf.m()` sets the death counter to 12, clears its moving occupancy rows y+1 through y+5, and removes linked `az`/`aj` immediately (bytecode 0–91). After the counter reaches zero, `cf.l()` writes value 1 to its source occupancy cells at x=6–9 and y through y+2 (0–65). This override does not call `au.l()`, so the source `cf` entity remains in its death state.
- The indexed records contain HP/attack values 14430/210 for enemy 39 and 5910/200 for enemies 40 and 41. The `/boss/spr/3_39` set has idle, movement, and attack tracks; `3_40` and `3_41` have idle and attack tracks but no separate movement track. The web uses those extracted track lengths for the corresponding update cadence.

## Web implementation

`addSourceConstructedMapEnemies()` now appends the three actors in source order with their source sprite slots, render keys, footprints, linked-group key, and draw offsets. `source-enemy-ai.ts` advances the two attacker patterns, the `cf` hazard rows and cooldown, attack damage and knockback metadata, the camera offsets, and the source death countdown. The scene uses the extracted boss tracks, excludes `az`/`aj` from movement occupancy as their constructors do, clears the linked actors when `cf` dies, retains the persistent `cf` corpse, and applies the final collision-only writes. Player half-tile occupancy is checked during the moving hazard sweep.

Focused tests cover spawn order and metadata, attack frames and ranges, attacker movement and camera shake, hazard sweeps and half-tile occupancy, knockback and tail damage, the rest period, final collision writes, persistent death state, and linked cleanup.

M144 cross-checks the custom update entry points for status handling: AZ and CF call `ak.n()` before custom movement/action work, so status 0/2 resets their action to idle; AJ calls `ak.n()` only outside state 3, so an active attack continues through its own frame callbacks. The web follows those branches, preserves the non-action fields, advances the map-15 lifetime counter before reset, and doubles cooldowns under status 1 at attack start/recovery. Unit tests cover the reset, status bypass, and cooldown cases.

M276 corrects AZ's successful-strike lifetime reset. `az.j()` resets its timer and action-delay field after an in-range frame-five strike callback; an out-of-range update leaves both alone. The web derives that mutable action delay from the lifetime field, so it now resets `lifetimeTicks` only when the callback runs. A focused unit test checks both hit and miss paths; this is static-source and unit-test evidence, not a natural combat replay or J2ME comparison.
